About

Who writes this, and why

A food manufacturer runs this site. That is worth knowing before you read anything else here, so it is on this page rather than buried in a footer.

The Malaysian Pantry is published by Tropicor Foods, a halal-certified food ingredient manufacturer based in Klang, Selangor. The company has been making seasonings, premixes, sauces and fruit ingredients for over thirty years, mostly for other businesses: restaurants, hotels, food manufacturers and brands that sell under their own name.

So there is an obvious question. Why would an ingredient manufacturer write about how to store belacan?

The honest answer

Two reasons, and neither is complicated.

The first is that we get asked. A lot. People email to ask whether an emulsifier is halal, how long a marinade actually needs, why their sambal split, what MESTI involves, whether they need their own factory to launch a product. Those answers were sitting in people's heads and in scattered email replies. Writing them down properly seemed more useful than answering the same question forty times.

The second is straightforwardly commercial. If you come to know Tropicor through something genuinely useful, and one day you need an ingredient supplier or a contract manufacturer, we would like you to think of us. That is the whole of it. We are not going to pretend otherwise on a page called About.

How we handle the conflict

A manufacturer writing about ingredients has an obvious bias. Here is how we try to keep it in check.

We write about the whole subject, not only the parts we sell into. Most of what is on this site has nothing to do with anything Tropicor makes. Nobody is buying candlenuts from us.

We say when the cheap option is fine. A lot of the time it is. An article that concludes every question with "buy a better product" is an advert wearing a hat.

We mention our own products rarely, and only where they are honestly one of the answers. When we do, they sit alongside the alternatives, with their limitations stated.

We do not invent expertise. Where someone at Tropicor is quoted, they said it. Where we are drawing on published standards or public guidance, we say which.

If you ever read something here that fails those tests, we would genuinely like to know.

What we write about

Five areas, roughly. Ingredients, meaning what things are and how to choose them. Technique, meaning why methods work rather than just what to do. Halal, which is a subject with an enormous amount of confident misinformation around it. Food business, for people running or starting something small. And culture, because a lot of Malaysian cooking only makes sense with the context attached.

The site is new, so not all of those sections are open yet. Ingredients and halal are live. The rest arrive as the writing does.

The bias throughout is towards Malaysian practice. There are plenty of good food sites explaining how things work in London or Los Angeles. Fewer that assume you are standing in a wet market in Klang wondering which pile of ikan bilis is worth the extra ringgit.

Corrections

Food writing gets things wrong, and regional food writing gets things wrong more often, because practice varies by state, by household and by decade. If something here is inaccurate, or true in Penang but not in Johor, tell us and we will fix it and say that we did.
